# Self-Study Aerospace Engineering Curriculum: Bachelor's (4 Years) + Master's (2 Years)

Oct 19, 2026 ·&#32;

## How to use this plan

This curriculum mirrors how a full-time student progresses through a 4-year B.Sc./B.Eng. in Aerospace Engineering (240 ECTS) followed by a 2-year M.Sc. (120 ECTS) at a top-tier university. The order follows programmes such as MIT AeroAstro: science and math core (Year 1) → engineering science (Year 2) → aerospace disciplines and lab (Year 3) → advanced analysis, electives and capstone design (Year 4).

- **Credits & load:** 1 ECTS ≈ 25–30 hours of total work. A 30-ECTS semester is \~750–900 h, i.e. \~40 h/week over a \~20-week term (15 teaching weeks plus exam and project weeks). At 20 h/week, expect double the calendar time.
- **Prerequisites:** as at a university, start a course only after passing its prerequisites. Key chains: calculus → differential equations → dynamics → stability & control; signals & systems → control → estimation; numerical methods → CFD/FEM; structures → structural dynamics → aeroelasticity.
- **Per course:** lectures → textbook chapter → weekly problem sets without looking at solutions → timed midterm and final using the posted exams. Set a pass mark (e.g., 60%) and repeat material you fail.
- **Format key:** (V) = video lectures; (N) = lecture notes, problem sets and exams, no video.
- **Links:** every course page was checked on 19 Oct 2026. If one moves, search the course number or title on the platform.
- **Books:** marked (free) are open-access; the rest are standard textbooks (library or purchase).

## Part B — Master's degree (2 years, 120 ECTS)

Entry assumes all of Part A, as a top-tier M.Sc. would: strong linear algebra, differential equations, control, CFD and FEM.

- **Structure:** a graduate core with one breadth course (M1), one specialisation track plus thesis proposal (M2-S1), and a 30-ECTS thesis (M2-S2).
- **Load:** graduate courses need \~8–10 h/week each; a 30-ECTS semester is still \~40 h/week.
- **Format:** many graduate MIT courses are notes + problem sets only (N). Pair them with the textbook and work every problem.
- **Output:** a Master's is judged by research. Aim to finish with one paper submitted to a conference or posted as a preprint.

### Master's Year 2, Semester 2 — Thesis (30 ECTS)

The thesis is a full semester (\~30 ECTS equivalent) of original work in your track.

1. **Question:** one falsifiable research question, narrowed from your literature log.
2. **Method:** analysis, simulation (CFD/FEM/dynamics) or experiment, with a written verification and validation plan before you run anything.
3. **Reproducibility:** public code and data repository, fixed versions, scripts that regenerate every figure.
4. **Results:** uncertainty or sensitivity analysis on every key number; compare with published data.
5. **Write-up:** thesis document (\~60–80 pages) plus a short paper version submitted to a conference or posted as a preprint.
6. **Review:** ask a practising engineer or academic to critique the draft before submission.

## Practical work and summers

As in a formal degree, summers are for hands-on work.

- **After Year 1:** design, build and trim a hand-launched glider; log flight data.
- **After Year 2:** size an RC aircraft from your Airplane Performance calculations, model it in [NASA OpenVSP](https://openvsp.org) (free), and compare predicted vs measured performance.
- **After Year 3:** internship or summer research placement with a university lab or company.
- **Year 4:** the capstone project above.
- **Code as you go (Python):** standard atmosphere, drag polar, range/endurance, Tsiolkovsky equation, two-body orbit propagator, longitudinal flight modes. Validate each against textbook examples, and keep all code and reports in a public repository from day one.

## Credentials and limits

Self-study builds knowledge, not a degree; most aerospace employers and engineering registration bodies expect an accredited degree.

- **NPTEL** offers proctored exams with certificates (small fee) for its courses — the closest route to verifiable credentials with free content. Coursera/edX certificates are paid.
- A public **portfolio** (GitHub code, design reports, built hardware) is the strongest evidence of competence for a self-taught engineer.
- Keep records of every course and exam score; part-time or distance degree programmes may credit prior learning later.
